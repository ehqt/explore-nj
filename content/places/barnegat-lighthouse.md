Barnegat Lighthouse stands at the northern end of Long Beach Island in Ocean County, in the borough of Barnegat Light. It is nicknamed "Old Barney." [@nrhp-nomination] [@borough-history] [@njht]

The tower was designed by George G. Meade, an Army engineer who held the rank of lieutenant at the time and who went on to lead Union forces at Gettysburg. Built of brick and iron, it has a spiral staircase built from an iron pipe with cast-iron treads, and the borough counts 217 steps. The borough gives January 1, 1859, as the date it was commissioned. The 1970 National Register nomination says it was built in 1857, and the park service inventory lists 1857 as the year the light was first lit and 1859 as the year its original lens was installed. Its height is also listed two ways: 163 feet above the land in the nomination, and 172 feet on the borough's site and in a National Park Service inventory archived in 2007. [@njht] [@nrhp-nomination] [@borough-history] [@nps-inventory]

The coast around the lighthouse has moved. An earlier, 40-foot brick lighthouse built for the station in 1834 or 1835 "toppled into the sea" in the 1850s, according to the park service inventory. The inlet itself was once half a mile north of the present tower; by 1970, the nomination says, it had reached the tower's base. [@nps-inventory] [@nrhp-nomination]

## Why it matters

The New Jersey Historic Trust says Old Barney and the Fire Island light, farther up the coast, worked as a pair to help ships find their way into New York harbor. The nomination counts it as one of four lighthouses built along the Jersey shore between Sandy Hook and Cape May, and credits them with guiding "innumerable ships" safely along the coast. [@njht] [@nrhp-nomination]

Sources disagree on when it stopped guiding ships. The 1970 nomination says it served until 1927, when a lightship anchored offshore took over; the park service inventory lists 1927 as the year it was automated and 1944 as the year it went dark, and the Historic Trust and the borough also give 1944. The Barnegat Light Museum, like the nomination, dates the decommissioning to 1927. The inventory adds that it served as a lookout for German submarines during World War II. On January 1, 2009, the beam was lit again. [@nrhp-nomination] [@nps-inventory] [@njht] [@borough-history] [@museum-collections]

The tower was closed to visitors in the summer of 1989 because it had deteriorated badly, and a 1990 grant from the New Jersey Historic Trust helped pay for structural repairs that let the public back in. Its original first-order Fresnel lens went to the Barnegat Light Museum, which received it in 1957. [@njht] [@nps-inventory] [@museum-home] [@museum-collections]

## Worth a visit?

Yes. The Borough of Barnegat Light describes Barnegat Lighthouse State Park as a popular place to climb the lighthouse, picnic and fish. The Barnegat Light Museum nearby, run by the local historical society, displays the tower's original lens. Check the state park's official site for current hours and access. [@borough-home] [@museum-home]

On West State Street in Trenton, a gold dome marks the building where New Jersey's laws are made. The State House holds the chambers of both the Senate and the General Assembly, and its tours take in the executive side of the building as well as the legislative one. [@njleg-legislature] [@tours-faq] [@tours-history]

A 2018 executive order stated that Trenton had been the state capital since the State House was built in 1792, and an undated Legislature brochure dates the building's role as the seat of state government to that year. The Legislature's tour office calls it "one of the oldest state capitols in the country," and the brochure called it one of the oldest "in continuous use." [@eo-40] [@house-today] [@tours-history]

The first State House, built by Jonathan Doane on a 3.75-acre site, had two and a half stories and a bell tower. Philadelphia architect John Notman added a wing and a domed rotunda in 1845, and a fire early on March 21, 1885 destroyed the wing facing West State Street. Lewis Broome of Jersey City designed its replacement, including the rotunda and dome that are still in place. [@tours-history]

## Why it matters

The building has been reshaped again and again. The brochure counts twelve major construction projects that changed its size and shape, and says a 1960s master plan would have torn the whole building down had it been carried out. The Assembly wing was rebuilt in 1891 and the Senate wing in 1903, and both have stayed intact since. [@house-today] [@tours-history]

The brochure says attention later turned to preserving and restoring the building. Work that began in 1987 restored the legislative side of the building, with craftspeople using stained glass, gilding and decorative molding to recreate the original details of the chambers and rotunda. A second project closed the front of the building in July 2017 and was completed in 2023, bringing the Rotunda and the governor's outer office back to how they looked in 1911 to 1913. [@house-today] [@tours-history]

Trenton's own history reaches back to the Revolution. New Jersey's official state history notes that some historians rank George Washington's 1776 victory at Trenton as "the most important American military victory ever." The 2018 executive order adds that Trenton briefly served as the capital of the United States in 1784, and it counted the state as the city's largest employer, with more than 16,000 state workers. [@nj-short-history] [@eo-40]

## Worth a visit?

Yes. The Legislature's Office of Legislative Services runs guided tours of the State House that cover its history, art and architecture and how laws are made, and visitors see the Senate and General Assembly chambers and the Rotunda. Tours must be reserved in advance and there are no self-guided visits. The New Jersey State Police screen every visitor, and adults need photo ID. Some areas can be closed during legislative business, so check the official site before you go. [@tours-visit] [@tours-faq]

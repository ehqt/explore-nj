The Atlantic City Boardwalk is a wooden promenade running along the beach in Atlantic City. It began in 1870 as a practical fix: by Visit Atlantic City's account, it was originally built "to keep sand out of hotel lobbies and train cars." The city's public library and its tourism bureau both describe it as the first boardwalk in the United States. [@vac-boardwalk-history] [@acfpl-faq]

According to the library's local history room, a petition for a footwalk on the beach reached City Council on April 25, 1870, and $5,000 was set aside to build it. The walk opened on June 26, 1870. It was a mile long and eight feet wide, and it sat roughly a foot over the sand. The library says that 25 years after it opened, Boardwalk became an official street name, which is why the word is capitalized there. [@acfpl-faq]

The walk was rebuilt several times, with new versions opening in 1880 and 1884 and dedicated in 1890 and 1896, and its herringbone plank pattern goes back to 1916. Before a hurricane in 1944, the library says, the Boardwalk ran about 7 miles, through Ventnor and Margate into Longport; its timeline records that the September 14, 1944 storm destroyed more than half of it. [@acfpl-faq] [@acfpl-history]

## Why it matters

The library's history describes Atlantic City as a major vacation resort from the 1880s to the 1940s, drawing people from nearby cities with inexpensive train service and a mix of lodging, amusement piers and floor shows. Rolling chairs were being rented out on the Boardwalk by 1884, Steel Pier opened in 1898, and the library says the term "salt water taffy" was coined on the Boardwalk in the 1880s. The same history says the resort's popularity began to fall off in the 1950s as air travel made Florida and the Caribbean easier to reach. [@acfpl-history] [@acfpl-faq]

The Miss America pageant grew out of a 1921 contest that local businessmen staged to keep visitors coming after Labor Day. Sixteen-year-old Margaret Gorman, representing Washington, D.C., won the first title. The Miss America Organization's own history says the icon was born "on the boardwalk of Atlantic City," and its 1940 timeline says more than 2,500 civic leaders rallied behind the Boardwalk Convention Hall as the pageant's "permanent new home." It did not stay there for good: the library notes the pageant was held in Las Vegas from 2006 to 2013. [@acfpl-miss-america] [@mao-history]

The board game Monopoly also has roots here. The National Toy Hall of Fame traces the game to Elizabeth Magie's Landlord's Game of 1904, says Pennsylvanian Charles Darrow produced the first commercial version in 1934, and notes that the original board used property names familiar to Atlantic City residents. The library says Darrow "is credited with inventing Monopoly as we know it" and that he sketched the city's streets on his kitchen tablecloth; in 1970 the city put up a plaque in his honor on the Boardwalk near Park Place. [@toy-hof-monopoly] [@acfpl-faq]

Casino gambling came after two statewide votes. New Jersey voters rejected a 1974 referendum, then in 1976 approved a narrower one that allowed casinos only within Atlantic City. The first casino, Resorts International, opened on May 26, 1978, in the converted Chalfonte-Haddon Hall. The library's history notes that critics doubted gambling as a tool for rebuilding the city while supporters expected casinos to fund its revival. A 2026 Visit Atlantic City page says the city is home to nine casino resorts. [@acfpl-casinos] [@ccc-history] [@acfpl-history] [@vac-boardwalk-history]

## Worth a visit?

Yes. Visit Atlantic City calls the Boardwalk "historic, wooden and five miles long," with the beach a few steps away and souvenir shops, restaurants and live entertainment along the way. Things it currently lists there include rides at Steel Pier, rolling chair trips, saltwater taffy, Boardwalk tram tours and The Atlantic City Experience in Jim Whelan Boardwalk Hall. Check the official site for current rules and opening times. [@vac-about] [@vac-beaches] [@vac-boardwalk-history]

Hollybush is a stone house on the campus of Rowan University in Glassboro. Rowan says Thomas Whitney, of the family that founded the Whitney Brothers Glassworks in 1842, built it after a tour of Europe. A 1971 nomination to the National Register of Historic Places records a datestone on the tower inscribed "Thomas H. Whitney A.D. 1849," names John Notman as the architect, and calls the house "an excellent example" of the Italian Villa style. [@rowan-mansion] [@nr-nomination]

The Whitney family kept the property until the early 1900s. After the land was split up, a group of local residents raised money to buy part of the estate, house included, and offered it to the state if Glassboro was chosen for a new teacher-training school. That school opened in 1923 and became Rowan University in 1992. The college renovated the mansion as a women's dormitory, and for many years it was the home of the school's presidents; President Herman James's family lived there until 1998. [@rowan-mansion] [@nr-nomination] [@glassboro-history]

On June 23 and June 25, 1967, President Lyndon B. Johnson and Soviet Premier Alexei Kosygin met in the house's library for close to 10 hours of talks. The meeting was tied to Kosygin's attendance at a special session of the United Nations General Assembly on the Middle East. According to Rowan, Governor Richard Hughes suggested the college as a compromise site, since Kosygin was unwilling to go to Washington and Johnson was unwilling to meet in New York City. Rowan says the college and the borough had just 16 hours to prepare, and thousands of people turned out along Glassboro's streets. [@rowan-summit] [@frus-summary]

## Why it matters

The two leaders discussed the Arab-Israeli Six-Day War, which had erupted in early June, along with the nuclear arms race and conflicts in Vietnam. They reached no agreements. The State Department's Office of the Historian calls the talks "inconclusive": Kosygin demanded an immediate, full withdrawal of Israeli troops and rejected Johnson's proposal on arms shipments to the region, and when Johnson pressed for arms control talks, Kosygin wanted the Middle East and Vietnam addressed first. [@frus-summary] [@rowan-summit] [@rowan-home]

On what the meeting did achieve, Rowan says the talks "helped temper Cold War tensions," and Johnson spoke of "the spirit of Hollybush." The LBJ Presidential Library says the friendly mood, called the "Spirit of Glassboro," "was seen to have improved relations," and its online collection lists a malachite box that Kosygin gave Johnson during the summit. The Office of the Historian adds that in the months afterward, relations between the two countries "remained essentially on dead center." [@rowan-summit] [@rowan-home] [@lbj-box] [@frus-summary]

The house was listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 1972. Its nomination, written in 1971, described the talks as "the beginning of a meaningful East-West dialogue." [@nr-nomination]

## Worth a visit?

Yes. Rowan offers regularly scheduled tours from September through April and lists tour dates for the 2026–2027 season; groups and requests for other days can be arranged. Rowan describes the house as a venue for university and community functions, with displays of artifacts from the 1967 summit. Rowan says major restoration began in 2003, and that a paint analysis of the interior found decorative ceiling painting in the parlor and library. Check the official page for current tour dates. [@rowan-tours] [@rowan-preserving] [@rowan-summit]

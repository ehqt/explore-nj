WheatonArts is an arts campus on 45 wooded acres in Millville, Cumberland County. At its heart is the Museum of American Glass, and the grounds also hold the Down Jersey Folklife Center and studios for hot glass, flamework and pottery, where artists demonstrate glassmaking and pottery for visitors. [@wa-visit] [@wa-about] [@wa-studios]

WheatonArts traces the American glass industry's start in southern New Jersey to the region's natural resources, among them wood, sand and silica. A New Jersey Historical Commission lesson adds clay for crucibles and water transport to the list. In 1739 Caspar Wistar, a Philadelphia merchant who had immigrated from Germany, opened a glassworks near Alloway in Salem County. WheatonArts calls it the nation's "earliest successful glass factory," and the Encyclopedia of Greater Philadelphia calls it the most successful glassmaking operation in colonial America. The encyclopedia adds that a number of colonial glassmakers learned the trade there, and many of them settled nearby. [@wa-history] [@njhc-wistarburgh] [@phila-encyclopedia]

Millville lies 25 miles from the Wistar site, and the Historical Commission's lesson calls it one of the nation's leading glass manufacturing centers since 1806. The Wheaton name comes from Theodore Corson Wheaton, a pharmacist who in 1888 began making his own medicine bottles at a Millville glass factory. His company grew into a multinational corporation. It incorporated as Wheaton Industries in 1971 and was sold in 1996 to Algroup, a Swiss aluminum company, which Canada's Alcan bought in 2000. After further mergers and acquisitions, the Millville business was operating in the late 2010s as DWK Life Sciences, with about two hundred local workers making laboratory products. [@njhc-wistarburgh] [@wa-history] [@phila-encyclopedia] [@bard-wheaton]

## Why it matters

In the early 1960s, Theodore Wheaton's grandson, Frank H. Wheaton Jr., visited the Corning Museum of Glass in Corning, New York, and found much of southern New Jersey's glass on display there. He felt such pieces should be shown in the region that made them. A collection of American glass he located at the Bucks County Glass Museum in Pennsylvania became the core of the new museum's holdings. [@wa-history]

The center was founded as Wheaton Village in 1968, and its first buildings opened to the public in 1970. The building that now houses the museum opened in December 1973. [@wa-about] [@wa-history] [@wa-museum]

WheatonArts describes its holdings as one of the most comprehensive collections of American glass in the world, spanning 18th-century glass to contemporary glass art. The center's own pages size the collection differently: "over 20,000 pieces" on the museum page and "over 22,000 objects" on its history page, while Bard Graduate Center gives "more than 22,000 objects." WheatonArts says the museum is one of only nine in New Jersey accredited by the American Alliance of Museums. [@wa-museum] [@wa-programs] [@wa-history] [@bard-wheaton]

Children worked in Millville's glass factories. In his 1904 piece "Millville," the poet Carl Sandburg wrote that "by day and by night, the fires burn on in Millville," and described the factories' "carryin-boys," who ranged from nine to eighteen years old. The Historical Commission explains that their job was to move half-finished and finished glassware around the factory floor. [@njhc-wistarburgh]

The center also supports working artists. In 1983 it created a program for emerging contemporary glass artists, now the Creative Glass Fellowship Program, which each year gives up to 10 emerging and mid-career artists residencies with round-the-clock studio access, materials, housing and stipends. [@wa-history] [@wa-programs]

## Worth a visit?

Yes, in season. The Glass Studio, a building inspired by the original 1888 T. C. Wheaton Glass Factory, hosts daily demonstrations, and the technique on show varies from day to day, from glassblowing to pouring glass into molds. In the museum, the exhibition "Transformations: The Wasserstein Collection of Contemporary Glass," a gift of almost 60 works, is scheduled to run through December 31, 2027. The campus closes to daily visitors for part of the year, so check the official site for current hours, tickets and events. [@wa-studios] [@wa-about] [@wa-visit]

The Great Falls of the Passaic sit in the middle of Paterson, where the Passaic River drops through a dark basalt gorge. The falls are the centerpiece of Paterson Great Falls National Historical Park, and they were named a National Natural Landmark in 1967. [@nps-nature]

The National Park Service describes Paterson as the first planned industrial city in the United States, which Alexander Hamilton set out to build here in 1792, using the river's power to run mills. The project was organized by the Society for Establishing Useful Manufactures, founded in 1791. Into the 20th century, Paterson's mills made cotton cloth, railroad locomotives, firearms, textile machinery and silk, and the city's nickname became "Silk City." [@nps-history] [@nps-nature]

Paterson has a long history of workers striking over their hours, pay and conditions, from a children's strike in 1835 to the Great Silk Strike of 1913. The city was built within Lenapehoking, the homeland of the Lenape, and it grew with immigrants, who still settle here today. [@nps-history]

## Why it matters

The park presents itself as a meeting point of natural and urban environments, where a thriving natural ecosystem survives in the heart of a busy city. [@nps-nature]

The rock of the gorge is basalt from lava flows roughly 190 to 200 million years ago, as the supercontinent Pangea split apart. Water has flowed over the falls for roughly 14,000 years, since a huge meltwater lake called Glacial Lake Passaic drained through here as the last Ice Age glacier retreated. [@nps-geology]

A hydroelectric plant beside the falls still generates power from the river, continuing the use of water power that Paterson was founded on. [@nps-nature]

## Worth a visit?

Yes. The falls are the heart of a national park in the middle of the city. The water is usually at its strongest around March and April. Check the park's official site for current hours, directions and programs. [@nps-faq] [@nps-nature]

Ocean Grove is a seaside town in Neptune Township, Monmouth County, lying between Wesley Lake and Fletcher Lake on the Atlantic coast, just south of Asbury Park. Its centerpiece is the Great Auditorium, a wooden hall opened in 1894; the town's 1975 National Register nomination says Ocean Grove "literally grew up around the Auditorium." [@nr-nomination] [@hsog-about] [@neptune-og] [@ogcma-history]

The town started as a Methodist camp meeting ground, a place for summer religious retreats. According to the 1975 nomination, the Rev. William B. Osborn picked the site after searching the New Jersey coast (the Association's own history describes a group of ministers searching in 1869), and on July 31, 1869, a small group camping there held their first prayer meeting. In December 1869, thirteen ministers and thirteen laymen formed the Ocean Grove Camp Meeting Association, and the New Jersey Legislature chartered it on March 3, 1870. The Association, which describes its setting as Christian, still holds summer Sunday worship in the Great Auditorium and runs the beach. [@nr-nomination] [@hsog-about] [@ogcma-history] [@ogcma-sundays] [@ogcma-beach]

The Association auctioned lots in 1870. James A. Bradley, a New York businessman, paid $86, the highest premium for any lot; he later developed the land to the north as Asbury Park. The streets were laid out so that the avenues widen as they near the sea and the buildings closer to the beach stand farther back, giving each house an ocean view; the 1975 nomination says Ocean Grove "appears to be the earliest user of this device." [@nr-nomination] [@hsog-about]

## Why it matters

The Great Auditorium is the fourth structure on its spot, after an 1870 preacher's stand and the roofed and enlarged versions that followed. Ground was broken on December 2, 1893, and the hall opened on July 1, 1894, its roof carried on trusses spanning 161 feet. The Association's history page says the hall originally held almost 10,000 and now seats 6,250 since cushioned theater-style seats were installed; its walking tour gives 9,600 originally and about 7,000 today. The Association also says the conductor Leonard Bernstein once compared its acoustics to Carnegie Hall's. [@nr-nomination] [@ogcma-history] [@ogcma-auditorium]

Around the Auditorium is a summer tent colony. The Historical Society says 660 tents once stood on lots across Ocean Grove, and that 114 are left. Behind each tent is a wooden structure with a kitchen and bathroom; each spring the canvas is put up over a wooden platform in front, and in the fall it comes down again. Neptune Township says the waiting list to lease one is long. [@hsog-about] [@ogcma-tents] [@neptune-og]

The 1975 nomination calls the town's architecture mainly stick style "on the builder-vernacular level," with porches on most houses and trim of local manufacture. It counted 754 plain stick-style structures, 164 in an Eastlake stick style, 198 Queen Anne and 118 shaped like the tents of the early camp. New Jersey listed the district on December 16, 1975, and the National Register followed on April 12, 1976. Neptune Township describes Ocean Grove as having "the largest assemblage of authentic Victorian architecture in the nation," and the Historical Society lists maintaining that status among its aims. [@nr-nomination] [@hpo-list] [@nr-record] [@neptune-og] [@hsog-home]

The Association set its own rules. The Historical Society lists bans on carriages and cars on Sundays and on Sunday sea bathing, and on selling liquor within a mile of the town. The Association's walking tour says that for Ocean Grove's first 110 years, driving, cycling, boating and swimming were not allowed on the Sabbath. In 1875, as the Historical Society tells it, President Ulysses S. Grant left his carriage at the gates on a Sunday and walked in. [@hsog-about] [@ogcma-founders] [@ogcma-auditorium]

The Association's legal powers have been tested in court. State laws dating back to 1870 had let its trustees, who had to be Methodists, make local rules and run a police force and a municipal court. In State v. Celmer, decided June 21, 1979, the New Jersey Supreme Court ruled the arrangement unconstitutional, because it handed government power to a religious body. The case began in 1976, when the Ocean Grove police arrested a driver who was then convicted in the Association's municipal court of drunk driving, speeding and disregarding a traffic signal. The Supreme Court held that the court had been improperly set up, and it ordered acquittals on all three charges rather than a new trial, saying a retrial would be unfair. The court said those government functions must instead be carried out by Neptune Township, and that the Association could keep rules that people follow voluntarily. [@celmer-1979]

A later dispute concerned the Boardwalk Pavilion, which the Association used for worship services, Bible school, gospel music and summer band concerts. When no event was scheduled it was open to the public, and it had been available for public rental for weddings, though the two sides disputed how far it had been rented for secular purposes. In March 2007 two Ocean Grove residents applied to rent it for their civil union ceremony. The Association refused, saying the use was not consistent with its religious beliefs. That couple, and a second couple who had also asked to hold a civil union ceremony there, complained to the New Jersey Division on Civil Rights. The Association then sued the Division's director in federal court, arguing that by accepting and investigating the complaints under the state's anti-discrimination law, the Division had violated its First Amendment rights to free speech, expressive association and free exercise of religion. In 2009 the Third Circuit upheld the dismissal of the Association's claims about the pavilion, leaving that question to the state proceedings, and returned its claims about the rest of its property to the lower court. While that appeal was pending, the Division found probable cause on the first couple's complaint but not on the second's. Under the state process, a probable-cause finding is followed by a hearing; the 2009 opinion does not report a final outcome. [@ca3-2009]

## Worth a visit?

Yes. Ocean Grove has a beach, a boardwalk and a main street of shops. In summer, the Association offers hosted weekday visits to the Great Auditorium and a self-guided walking tour of its landmarks, and the Historical Society runs a museum on Pitman Avenue. Check the Association's official site for current events and visiting details. [@ogcma-history] [@ogcma-tour] [@hsog-home] [@ogcma-beach]

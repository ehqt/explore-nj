Branch Brook Park is a long, thin strip of parkland in Newark's North Ward, lying between the Forest Hill and Roseville neighborhoods, and a 1920s land gift carried its northern end into Belleville. Essex County puts it at 360 acres and nearly four miles from end to end, running from U.S. Route 280 in Newark up to Mill Street in Belleville, and roughly a quarter mile wide on average. The 1980 National Register nomination gave a shorter length of about two miles. The Newark Light Rail runs along the park's west side. [@ecp-about] [@ecp-faq] [@nr-1980]

The park is named for the Branch Brook, which the county describes as a Passaic River tributary running through a shallow valley here, and that stream became the basis for the park's chain of waterways: open lakes at the southern end, a winding channel through the middle, and ponds and streams in the north. The 1980 nomination also describes the Second River flowing through the park's Northern Extension, where the most cherry trees were concentrated. [@ecp-about] [@ecp-faq] [@nr-1980]

Much of the land started out as a marsh that the county's history calls Old Blue Jay Swamp, hemmed in by run-down housing that the county describes as unhealthy. In 1895 Newark turned over about 60 acres around an old reservoir to the new Essex County Park Commission, and that parcel became the park's core. Grading began in June 1896; the swamp was drained, the houses were torn down, and by 1900 a lake, gardens and lawns had taken its place. The early plans, by John Bogart and Nathan F. Barrett, leaned on geometric, formal gardens. The Olmsted Brothers landscape firm was then hired to revise them; the 1980 nomination gives the hiring date as August 22, 1898, and The Cultural Landscape Foundation dates the Olmsted Brothers' revision to 1898, while Essex County's history says the firm was retained in 1900. Under the Olmsted revision, the south end around the old reservoir stayed formal, while the north end was given a looser, more natural layout with curving paths and roads. Both Essex County and the foundation say Frederick Law Olmsted Sr. first envisioned a park here in 1867. [@ecp-about] [@ecp-faq] [@nr-1980] [@tclf]

## Why it matters

Essex County calls Branch Brook the first county park in the United States to open for public use. The 1980 National Register nomination puts it a little differently: it describes the park as one of the original parks opened by the Essex County Park Commission, set up in 1895, which it calls the nation's first county park system. The park was added to the New Jersey Register of Historic Places in 1980 and the National Register in 1981. [@ecp-about] [@nr-1980] [@tclf]

The park is noted for its cherry trees. According to the nomination, Caroline Bamberger Fuld donated some 2,050 Japanese cherry trees for the park in 1927; The Cultural Landscape Foundation dates her gift to 1928. Essex County says the park has the largest collection of cherry blossom trees in the United States, as the 1980 nomination also claimed, while the foundation describes it as the largest on the East Coast. Tree counts vary as well: Essex County's own pages say more than 4,000 in one place and more than 5,000 in another, and the foundation says more than 4,000. [@ecp-about] [@ecp-faq] [@nr-1980] [@tclf]

Essex County's history says the Morris Canal, which ran beside the park, was abandoned and became the Newark subway, and the 1980 nomination likewise noted that the subway line along the park followed the old canal bed. [@ecp-about] [@nr-1980]

## Worth a visit?

Yes. It is a public Essex County park. The county says the cherry trees blossom in April and lists an annual Cherry Blossom Festival. The Newark Light Rail has a Branch Brook Park stop. Check the county's park site for current hours, events and directions. [@ecp-about] [@ecp-faq] [@ecp-park]

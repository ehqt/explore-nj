Watchung Reservation is a wooded Union County park, and the county describes it as its largest. It lies along the First and Second Watchung Mountains, with the valley of the Blue Brook between them, and it stretches three miles in length and one mile across. County pages give different figures for its size, among them 2,065 acres on Trailside's welcome page and 2,142 acres on its trails page. [@uc-camp-2026] [@uc-adopt-trail] [@trailside-welcome] [@trailside-trails]

According to the county, the park's name comes from "Wachunk," a Lenape word for "high hills." Most of the land was acquired in the 1920s, with the aim of keeping the whole tract "in its largely primeval state" and without "the polish or finish of a city park." The reservation is part of a county park system designed by the Olmsted Brothers, a landscape architecture firm from Brookline, Massachusetts. [@trailside-trails] [@uc-history-trail]

The pin marks the Trailside Nature & Science Center, which is Union County's environmental education center. It started in 1941 as the Trailside Museum, in a corner of a nursery building; a planetarium opened in 1969 at the newly renamed Trailside Nature and Science Center, and a visitor center opened there in 1974. The building was renovated in September 2006. Inside are a library, a 250-seat auditorium and 4,500 square feet of hands-on exhibits, with an exhibit built around a 34-foot American beech tree filling the atrium. [@trailside-welcome] [@uc-timeline]

## Why it matters

The National Park Service places Garret Mountain and the Great Falls in Paterson in "a series of ridges known as the Watchung Mountains," and it explains that lava flows, which came with the breakup of the supercontinent Pangaea, created those ridges. Union County's geology trail page says the reservation's rocks date from about 200 million years ago, in the late Triassic period. By its account, lava welled up three separate times over several million years, and the sand and mud that settled between those flows hardened into sandstone and shale. The same page says Ice Age glaciers "did not travel much further south than" the present-day reservation. [@nps-geology] [@uc-geology]

Inside the reservation is the Deserted Village of Feltville. David Felt, who had a stationery business in New York City, made the first of 28 transactions for land and water rights here on December 1, 1844, then built a mill along the Blue Brook and a village on the bluff above it to house the mill's workers. A 1979 nomination to the National Register calls Feltville "an example of a planned mid-nineteenth century company mill town." The county says 175 people were living there by 1850; the nomination says the 1850 census counted 173. [@uc-feltville] [@nrhp-feltville]

Felt sold the village in 1860. Later business ventures there failed, and during a period when the town may have stood empty, it became known as the Deserted Village. Warren Ackerman bought it at a public auction in 1882 and made it into a summer resort called Glenside Park, which closed in 1916. The nomination says the Union County Park Commission bought the area in 1927 and added it to the reservation. The county says the site was listed on the State and National Registers of Historic Places in 1980, and that some of its houses are inhabited. [@uc-feltville] [@nrhp-feltville]

## Worth a visit?

Yes. The county lists more than 26 miles of bridle trails and about 13 miles of marked hiking trails in the reservation. A six-mile History Trail, marked with pink blazes, starts and ends at Trailside and can also be joined from parking areas at the Deserted Village and Lake Surprise. Trailside's grounds also have a Sensory Friendly Trail, which opened in 2018; the county's page says it is currently under construction. At Feltville, a walking tour of about a mile covers the village's history and buildings. Check the county's official pages for current hours and programs.

Asbury Park's oceanfront is what the city calls a "mile of sand and water," and its boardwalk is home to music, restaurants, shops, mini golf, playgrounds and a splash park. The website of AP Boardwalk, the privately owned and operated venues and shops on the boardwalk, dates the city to 1871, when manufacturer James Bradley founded it as a "seaside residential resort," and says that soon afterward a boardwalk followed, with a pier, a pavilion for an orchestra and public changing rooms. [@city-beach] [@apb-about]

On the boardwalk stands the complex commonly referred to as "Convention Hall." A National Register nomination, which the Park Service's records date to 1979, describes it as two buildings joined by a roofed stretch of boardwalk 60 feet wide: Convention Hall and its pier, reaching east from the boardwalk, and the Paramount Theater beside Ocean Avenue. The New York firm Warren and Wetmore designed it. The city's commissioners approved a $2 million bond ordinance for it in June 1926, construction estimates were above $3 million when bids came in during January 1927, and the nomination says the hall was completed in 1929; the city gives 1930 as the year it officially opened. [@nr-nomination] [@nr-npgallery] [@city-cafe]

The nomination describes a troubled start. Skeptics called the hall Asbury Park's "white elephant," and as the Depression set in the city came close to municipal bankruptcy under a $10 million debt, "most of which was traceable to the beachfront." The U.S. Army Signal Corps and the Navy used the hall for wartime training from 1942 to 1946. Afterward it hosted Big Band dances with the bands of Tommy Dorsey, Jimmy Dorsey, Woody Herman and Glenn Miller, among others, along with skating shows, boxing and basketball, and in the decade before the nomination, rock concerts. [@nr-nomination]

## Why it matters

The nomination calls the complex "an unusually fine example of 1920's eclecticism employing Italian and French designs," decorated with seashells, dolphins and seahorses, four copper sailing ships, nautical lanterns and cast stone anchors. The city describes Convention Hall as "one of Asbury Park's most recognizable landmarks," and says it has been a venue for nearly a century of concerts, stage shows, sporting events, conventions and civic gatherings. [@nr-nomination] [@city-cafe]

The Stone Pony, a rock club on Ocean Avenue, opened on February 8, 1974, founded by Jack Roig and Robert "Butch" Pielka. The club's own history links it to Bruce Springsteen, Southside Johnny and the Asbury Jukes, Steven Van Zandt and members of the E Street Band, and to what it calls "the Sound of Asbury Park"; the website of the privately run AP Boardwalk credits the Stone Pony, the Wonder Bar, the Paramount and Convention Hall with helping create a genre it calls "The Jersey Shore Sound." [@pony-history] [@apb-about]

The Stone Pony's history states that Springsteen "did not begin his career" there. By the time it opened he was already a Columbia Records artist, and the Rock & Roll Hall of Fame lists his first two albums, Greetings from Asbury Park, N.J. and The Wild, the Innocent and the E Street Shuffle, as 1973 releases. The club says he began appearing on its stage in its early years and has come back many times since. The Library of Congress added his 1975 album Born to Run to its National Recording Registry in 2003. [@pony-history] [@rockhall-springsteen] [@loc-nrr]

Convention Hall and the Paramount have been closed since about 2021 because of structural safety concerns, according to the city. On August 20, 2026, the New Jersey Economic Development Authority approved a tax credit award of up to $75 million toward a $130 million restoration of the complex, which aims to reopen it as a year-round performing arts center. The city's August 2026 announcement says construction began in April at the developer's own risk, with a 34-month construction timeline expected following the board's approval. [@city-cafe] [@city-preservation]

## Worth a visit?

Yes, for the boardwalk itself, which the private AP Boardwalk website says is open year-round, with live music and other events throughout the year. The city says the Convention Hall complex, including Convention Hall, the Paramount Theater, the Casino Arcade and the Carousel building, is closed for now, so check the city's and AP Boardwalk's sites for what is open, and the Stone Pony's calendar for shows.

Oak Tree Road runs through Edison Township and into Iselin, a section of Woodbridge Township. A 2024 public-television documentary describes the mile-and-a-half stretch from Edison Township to Iselin as "the heart, the hub" of a "thriving Indian American and South Asian American business and professional community." A 2024 article in Kairos, the undergraduate magazine of Rutgers University's journalism department, says the area is "often dubbed 'Little India'" for its long strip of businesses owned by South Asians, and a speaker in the documentary's opening montage uses the same name. [@pbs-destination] [@kairos-2024]

Woodbridge Township's 2018 plan for the Iselin end lists the road as one of four downtown business districts in the township and notes its long role as Iselin's main shopping street. According to the plan, businesses serving South Asian customers had multiplied over the previous two decades, and it calls the road a "unique commercial center for south Asian food, fashion, and jewelry" and a "widely popular destination" for South Asians from across the metropolitan area. [@woodbridge-rehab-plan]

The documentary traces the road's history back more than 300 years, to when it was called the road from Woodbridge to the mountains. In its account, immigrants from India began settling along the road in Iselin and Edison during the 1970s, and in the early to mid-1980s new businesses took the place of boarded-up establishments as the corridor revived. The documentary also recounts that the early Indian-owned businesses faced repeated vandalism and harassment. In the film, the head of the local NAACP chapter recalls that in 1989 the National Conference of Christians and Jews and the prosecutor at the time were alarmed by the number of bias incidents on the road, and that officials were at first slow to treat them seriously. Business owners chose not to press charges against the perpetrators, most of whom were juveniles, a decision the film credits with helping to bring the communities together. [@pbs-destination]

## Why it matters

The India Day Parade, which the Indian Business Association hosts to celebrate India's Independence Day, is held along Oak Tree Road between Edison and Iselin, according to the documentary. News India Times reported that the association's 22nd parade took place across Iselin and Edison on August 9, 2026, and that organizers put the crowd at about 50,000. [@pbs-destination] [@nit-parade-2026]

According to the 2024 documentary, major festivals such as Diwali, Eid and Christmas bring large crowds to the street, and Pakistan Day celebrations have more recently been held there too. It also reported that Sri Lankan, Bangladeshi and Pakistani communities, among others, were setting up new establishments in the area. [@pbs-destination]

The Census Bureau's American Community Survey estimates for 2020 to 2024 count 42,521 Edison residents (margin of error 2,107) in the survey's Asian Indian group, within its table of people who are Asian alone, out of a township population of 108,164. For Woodbridge Township the figures are 16,352 (margin of error 1,517) out of 104,355. These cover each whole township, not just the road. [@acs-2024]

In his March 2025 State of the Township address, Woodbridge Mayor John E. McCormac called the Iselin district "one of the largest Asian Indian shopping and dining districts in the country." He said the township had opened new parking lots near the road and was upgrading traffic lights, sidewalks and curbs. Woodbridge also set up an Oak Tree Road Special Improvement District in 1991, by ordinance, to add parking for the stretch of businesses between Route 27 and Wood Avenue. [@woodbridge-state-of-township-2025] [@woodbridge-sid]

Rutgers landscape-architecture students studied the Iselin stretch in a senior design studio, which the township's 2018 plan dates to fall 2012. Their project page points out that the shops and street life sit within a 1950s residential neighborhood, which strains parking, sidewalks and stormwater drainage. New Jersey's list of fiscal year 2025 Transportation Alternatives grants includes $1,499,000 to Middlesex County for pedestrian safety improvements on Oak Tree Road, County Route 604, in Edison and Woodbridge. [@rutgers-studio] [@woodbridge-rehab-plan] [@njdot-ta-2025]

## Worth a visit?

Yes. Woodbridge Township's 2018 plan called the road a widely popular destination, and the mayor's 2025 address describes the district's enormous growth. The township publishes a page listing parking lots near the road for shoppers and diners, along with street parking on Oak Tree Road between Correja Avenue and Middlesex Avenue; check it before you go. [@woodbridge-rehab-plan] [@woodbridge-state-of-township-2025] [@woodbridge-parking]

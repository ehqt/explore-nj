Garret Mountain is part of the Watchung Mountains, the chain of ridges that also holds the Great Falls in Paterson. The National Park Service explains that lava flows formed these ridges, a result of the breakup of the supercontinent Pangaea, and it dates the flow that includes the falls to roughly 190 million years ago. Quarries once cut traprock, a name for the basalt, from the edges of these ridges, including in what is now Woodland Park. [@nps-geology]

On top of the ridge is Garret Mountain Reservation, a Passaic County park in Woodland Park and Paterson. The county describes 310 acres of recreational space more than 500 feet above sea level, with views of Paterson and the New York City skyline, and credits the Olmsted Brothers firm with the original landscape design. According to the county's project page, as archived in May 2026, the reservation opened to the public in July 1931. [@county-park] [@county-project]

Inside the reservation stands Lambert Castle. Catholina Lambert, who came to America from England and made his money in silk, had it built in 1892–93 and gave it the name "Belle Vista." In 1896 he added a 70-foot observation tower and a 100-by-35-foot art gallery. Lambert, whose firm was Dexter, Lambert and Co., had put up a three-story silk factory in Paterson in 1862, and the nomination that put the castle on the National Register in 1976 counts Lambert among Paterson's largest 19th-century silk manufacturers. [@county-park] [@county-castle] [@nrhp-nomination]

## Why it matters

The nomination says the house "symbolized his wealth and importance." It also quotes an 1897 New York Times description of the castle on "the ridge of hills" above Paterson, looking across the valley of the Passaic and Hackensack Rivers toward Manhattan. [@nrhp-nomination]

Lambert later lost much of his fortune. The Historical Society ties the firm's money troubles to "the labor movement of 1913" and says the firm was liquidated in 1914. In 1916 Lambert's art collection was auctioned to pay debts, though he lived in the castle until he died in 1923. His son Walter sold it to Paterson in 1925, the city used it for some years as a tuberculosis hospital, and in 1928 it passed to the Passaic County Park Commission, as part of what the county's castle page calls the 575-acre Garret Mountain Reservation; the county's park page now gives a figure of 310 acres of recreational space. [@pchs-lambert] [@county-castle] [@county-park]

The Passaic County Historical Society opened a museum and library in the castle in 1934. After several years of restoration work, the castle is again open for guided tours, which the county lists on its tourism site. The county's project page, as archived in May 2026, said the County of Passaic would operate and maintain the museum and park once the site reopened, and that a new archives and library building was planned as the Historical Society's new home. The 1896 observation tower is known as Lambert Tower, and the New Jersey Historic Trust says the castle's National Register nomination was amended in 2021 to include it and the carriage house. [@county-castle] [@county-project] [@njht-castle] [@nrhp-nomination]

## Worth a visit?

Yes. The county lists hiking trails, Barbour Pond, a scenic overlook, an equestrian center and Lambert Tower among the reservation's features, though it says the trail from the castle to the tower is closed for an indefinite period. Castle visits are guided tours of the period rooms and exhibit spaces, and photography is prohibited on them. Check the county's official pages for current hours, tickets and closures. [@county-park] [@county-castle]

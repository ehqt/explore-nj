The Salem Oak was a white oak that grew in the Salem Friends Burial Ground, the Quaker cemetery on Broadway in Salem City. It came down around 6 p.m. on June 6, 2019. Accounts of the cause differ: a member of the local Friends meeting told the Inquirer it was "stem failure," the city's history page says its roots gave way, and 6abc reported that the cause was unclear. The fallen trunk showed rot that had worked through it from the inside. [@inquirer-2019] [@pym-memorial] [@salem-city] [@wpvi-2019]

Estimates of its age vary. The Inquirer reported in 2019 that Salem County put it at 565 years, while the Salem Friends' memorial and the county's website now give an estimated 600 years. The Inquirer puts the usual life expectancy of the species at 200 to 300 years. [@inquirer-2019] [@pym-memorial] [@salem-county]

Salem dates from 1675, when John Fenwick, an English Quaker, established it. A treaty is part of the tree's story: tradition places Fenwick and the Lenni-Lenape in its shade, where they are said to have agreed to a treaty. The city and Quaker writers call the story a legend, the Inquirer calls it local tradition, and the county says only that the oak "is said to be" the treaty site. [@salem-city] [@pym-memorial] [@salem-county] [@inquirer-2019] [@pym-2023]

## Why it matters

Salem City's history page describes the oak as having been the city's "centerpiece." The Salem Friends meeting, which its 2019 memorial names as owner of the property and the tree, looked after the tree at what they called "great expense": from the 1950s, an arborist company they contracted treated it for insects, fertilized it, and fitted it with cables to hold up its branches and a lightning protection system. [@salem-city] [@pym-memorial] [@inquirer-2019]

According to the Friends' memorial, the state Department of Environmental Protection declared it New Jersey's largest white oak in 2016, when it stood 103 feet tall with a trunk 22 feet 4 inches around. The Inquirer also reports the 2016 title, and adds that by 2019 a larger white oak discovered in Morris County had moved it to second place on the state's Big Tree Register. [@pym-memorial] [@inquirer-2019]

The tree lives on through its offspring. A seedling called the Centennial Oak was planted in a Presbyterian cemetery in Salem (in 1875, by the Friends' account; in 1876, to mark the nation's centennial, by the Inquirer's). Another was planted along the George Washington Memorial Parkway in Virginia in 1932, for Washington's 200th birthday. Acorns that Salem Meeting had collected a year before the fall were grown into seedlings with help from the New Jersey Forest Service, and in November 2019 state officials announced that each of New Jersey's 565 municipalities would receive a seedling. [@pym-memorial] [@inquirer-2019] [@wpvi-2019] [@pym-2023]

Lenape people are part of New Jersey today. The Nanticoke Lenni-Lenape Tribal Nation, in New Jersey, traces its history in its homeland back more than 10,000 years; its citizens descend from Nanticoke and Lenape people who stayed or returned after many relatives "suffered removals and forced migrations" west and into Canada. In their 2019 memorial for the oak, Salem Friends wrote that Salem's Quakers have kept "a strong relationship" with the Lenni Lenape of the area. [@nlltribe] [@pym-memorial]

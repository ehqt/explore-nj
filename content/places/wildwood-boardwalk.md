The Wildwoods are three resort towns, Wildwood, North Wildwood and Wildwood Crest, on a barrier island off the southern New Jersey coast in Cape May County. Their boardwalk runs north from Cresse Avenue to 15th Avenue in North Wildwood, a stretch the regional tourism authority counts as 38 blocks. Along it the authority lists three amusement piers with more than 100 rides and attractions, three water parks by the beach, arcades, carnival games, shops, restaurants and a yellow tramcar. Its pages give the length two ways: "almost 2.5 miles" in one place and 1.85 miles in another. [@nr-mpdf] [@gw-boardwalk] [@gw-general]

A National Register document on the area's motels, received by the state in 2003, says visitors could walk on the first versions of a boardwalk, in Holly Beach and Anglesea, by 1888; Anglesea became North Wildwood in 1906, and Holly Beach merged with Wildwood in 1912. The tourism authority instead traces the boardwalk to "a mere 150-yard stretch of boards in the 1890s." While other shore towns fought erosion, the same document says, Wildwood's beach kept gaining sand, so the boardwalk had to be moved eastward "a number of times"; the authority says it moved closer to the ocean twice. [@nr-mpdf] [@gw-boardwalk]

After World War II, the boardwalk's amusements drew growing numbers of visitors. The motels document, quoting a local history, says that by 1960 "five major amusement centers" on Wildwood's boardwalk together "offered 116 amusement rides," and that the town's many nightclubs had earned it the nickname "Little Las Vegas" by the mid-1950s; it names Bill Haley and the Comets, Louis Prima and Peggy Lee among the performers who played the Wildwoods. The tourism authority says "Rock Around the Clock" "was first performed live in the Wildwoods at the HofBrau Hotel in 1954," and the Doo Wop Preservation League says Chubby Checker's "The Twist" was "first introduced to the world" at Wildwood's Rainbow Club in 1960. [@nr-mpdf] [@gw-general] [@dwpl-what]

## Why it matters

The motels document ties the postwar motel boom to the Garden State Parkway. Of the roughly 275 motels it counted in the three towns "in essentially unaltered condition," only about thirty were built before 1955, while as many as 180 went up between 1955 and 1965. In Wildwood Crest, motels were not allowed east of Atlantic Avenue until 1954, but by 1956 most of the land near its beach was open to them. A decline that began in the 1970s, the document says, meant less pressure to renovate or demolish the motels than in other places with roadside architecture, and it notes that the historian Thomas Hine named the Wildwoods one of two resorts in the country that preserve "concentrations of Populuxe motels." [@nr-mpdf]

The tourism authority says the motels' style, like the music of the time, became known nationally as "Doo Wop." The Doo Wop Preservation League, a nonprofit founded in 1997, describes it as "angular elements, space-age imagery, tropical themes and colors," set off by neon signs. The league says the Wildwoods hold "the largest collection of mid-20th century resort architecture" in America, the tourism authority makes a similar claim, and the authority's history page says the Wildwoods "are considered the Doo Wop Capital of the World." The league credits the architect Steven Izenour, who led studies of the island with the University of Pennsylvania, Yale and Kent State in the late 1990s, with inspiring the movement; in a study text the league publishes, credited to Izenour and the three universities, the '50s motels are compared to Cape May's Victorian "painted ladies." [@dwpl-home] [@dwpl-what] [@dwpl-heroes] [@dwpl-izenour] [@gw-historical] [@gw-history]

The league's district page places its "Doo Wop Motel District" mainly in Wildwood Crest, along roughly two miles between Atlantic and Ocean avenues, and says the State of New Jersey officially recognizes it as the "Wildwoods Shore Resort Historic District." The state Historic Preservation Office's register list, last updated in December 2024, shows that district (also called the "Do Wop Historic District") with only a "SHPO Opinion" dated July 23, 2003, and no New Jersey or National Register listing. The league's undated page counts more than 50 vintage motels in the district, down from nearly 100 in the late 1990s, and says several notable motels were demolished between 2000 and 2005. [@dwpl-district] [@hpo-list]

The state list dates the "Motels of the Wildwoods" documentation to March 25, 2004 on the National Register, and shows motels individually listed under it, including the Chateau Bleu Motel in North Wildwood, listed in 2004, and the Caribbean Motel at 5600 Ocean Avenue in Wildwood Crest, listed in August 2005. The motel's 2005 National Register nomination dates it to about 1957 or 1958 and credits it to the Morey Brothers, describing a tropical theme, plastic palms by the pool and a large red neon "Caribbean" sign in cursive letters. The borough's lodging list still includes it. [@hpo-list] [@nr-caribbean] [@nr-caribbean-record] [@crest-lodging]

The motels document says Wildwood's unofficial 20th-century motto, "World's Finest and Safest Bathing Beach," put its broad sands first, and the City of Wildwood describes a "wide, free, sandy beach." The tourism authority says the five miles of beach need no beach tags, and it calls them "the widest, safest, FREE beaches on the East Coast" on one page and "the widest, cleanest and safest beaches on the New Jersey Shore" on another. [@nr-mpdf] [@city-home] [@gw-beaches] [@gw-general] [@gw-beach-info]

## Worth a visit?

Yes. The tourism authority says the boardwalk itself is open year-round, while its shops, restaurants and attractions keep their own hours and may operate only seasonally. In a notice dated September 28, 2026, the City of Wildwood said construction would keep the sections from Hand Avenue to Cresse Avenue, and from Oak Avenue to Maple Avenue, closed to the public until May 8, 2027, the Oak-to-Maple section possibly sooner. For the motels, the Doo Wop Preservation League runs neon-night and architecture trolley tours from its Doo Wop Experience museum at 4500 Ocean Avenue; check its site and the tourism authority's for current details.

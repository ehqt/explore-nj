Ellis Island lies in New York Harbor. By the Supreme Court's measure, it is 1,300 feet off the Jersey City shore and a mile from Manhattan's southern tip. From 1892 to 1924 it was, in the National Park Service's words, "America's largest and most active immigration station," where more than 12 million immigrants were processed. The park says millions of Americans have ancestors who came through it. [@scotus-1998] [@nps-history] [@nps-home]

The island began small. An 1834 compact between New York and New Jersey, approved by Congress, drew the states' boundary down the middle of the Hudson River but made Ellis Island, then about three acres, part of New York even though it lay on New Jersey's side of that line. Once the federal government picked the island for its immigration station in the early 1890s, it kept filling in the surrounding shallows for about four decades. The Court counted roughly 24.5 acres of new land in all. Immigration was moved off the island in 1954. [@scotus-1998]

New Jersey took New York to the U.S. Supreme Court in 1993, arguing over who controlled the new land. On May 26, 1998, the Court ruled that "New Jersey does": the filled land is New Jersey's, while New York keeps the original island, plus the spot covered by an 1834 pier. The Court sent the case back to the court-appointed special master to propose a decree setting out the line. He had suggested bending the line so the main immigration building and the ground around it stayed in New York, but the Court said it had no power to redraw a boundary set by the compact. It acknowledged that the result "divides not just an island but some of the buildings on it." [@scotus-1998]

## Why it matters

Even Justice Breyer wrote that he had assumed Ellis Island was "part and parcel of New York," but the Court gave New Jersey all of the roughly 24.5 acres of filled land, leaving New York the original island, which was about three acres in 1834, and the old pier. The National Park Service lists Ellis Island under both New Jersey and New York, and the park's mailing address is in Jersey City. [@scotus-1998] [@nps-home]

The Court said that any tidier boundary would have to be worked out by the two states themselves, with the consent of Congress. [@scotus-1998]

## Worth a visit?

Yes. The island is part of Statue of Liberty National Monument and is home to the Ellis Island National Museum of Immigration. The Great Hall, where millions of immigrants were inspected, was restored in 1986. Getting there still means a boat ride, as it did for the immigrants. [@nps-home] [@nps-visit]

Ferries leave from The Battery in Lower Manhattan and from Liberty State Park in Jersey City, and one ticket covers both Ellis Island and Liberty Island. The National Park Service says Statue City Cruises is the only authorized ticket seller and ferry operator, and it recommends buying tickets in advance. Check the park's official site for current ferry and exhibit information before you go. [@nps-directions] [@nps-visit] [@nps-home]

Old Queens is a brownstone building at the center of what became Queen's Campus, the historic core of Rutgers in New Brunswick. John McComb, a New York City architect, drew it in the Federal style. College trustees laid the cornerstone in 1809. By 1811 it already held Queen's College, the New Brunswick Theological Seminary and a grammar school, and in 1825, once building work was done, the college moved in fully. [@ipo-old-queens]

The school itself dates to 1766, when the Dutch Reformed Church sought and won a royal charter for Queen's College. Its name honored Queen Charlotte, wife of King George III. In 1825 the trustees renamed it Rutgers College after Colonel Henry Rutgers, a benefactor and Revolutionary War patriot who also enslaved people. The campus block sits within Lenapehoking, the traditional territory of the Lenni-Lenape. [@ipo-queens-campus] [@rutgers-history]

Rutgers describes itself as the country's "eighth oldest institution of higher learning" and counts itself among just nine colleges that date from the colonial era, before the Revolution. Its Rutgers Scientific School was set up in 1864 as New Jersey's land-grant school, and the Rutgers Law of 1956 affirmed its role as the State University. Today it is The State University of New Jersey, a public research university. [@rutgers-history] [@rutgers-about]

## Why it matters

In its early years the building's central section held the whole school: meals were cooked and served on the ground floor, and classes, books and chapel services filled the stories above, while faculty lived in the wings at either end. The cupola went up to hold a bell that Henry Rutgers gave. The building holds administrative offices today. [@ipo-old-queens]

According to Rutgers, only seven of the nine colonial colleges still have their first building standing, and Old Queens is one of them. The National Park Service lists it as a National Historic Landmark, designated on May 11, 1976, and it is a contributing building in the Queen's Campus historic district, which joined the National Register of Historic Places in 1973. [@ipo-old-queens] [@nps-nhl-list] [@ipo-queens-campus]

The university's own Scarlet and Black Project, which grew out of a committee formed in 2015, researches the legacy of slavery and dispossession at Rutgers, and it says enslaved laborers worked to build the university. Drawing on that research, Rutgers named the walkway between Old Queens and Voorhees Mall "Will's Way," after an enslaved man who helped lay the building's foundation. It was dedicated in 2017. [@scarlet-black-about] [@rutgers-landmarks]

## Worth a visit?

Yes, for the grounds. Rutgers says several gates "welcome visitors" to its historic campus. The building itself holds administrative offices, so expect to see it from outside. Rutgers admissions also offers self-guided campus tours through the Rutgers–New Brunswick app that can be taken in person; check the official site for current options. [@rutgers-landmarks] [@rutgers-tours] [@ipo-old-queens]

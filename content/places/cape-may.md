Cape May sits at the southern tip of New Jersey, at the end of the Cape May Peninsula. The city calls itself "The Nation's Oldest Seashore Resort," and the National Park Service's landmark nomination says it has "a substantial claim" to that title. The Cape May Historic District, a National Historic Landmark recognized for its concentration of Victorian buildings, covers the city apart from its Coast Guard property. [@nhl-nomination] [@city-about] [@nhl-list]

The land was home to the Kechemeche, a band of the Lenape, before Europeans arrived, and the town is named for the Dutch captain Cornelius Jacobsen Mey. The city dates its life as a resort to 1766, when visitors from Philadelphia began coming for their health and pleasure. Through the 1800s it drew the famous and wealthy: Henry Clay visited in 1847, several presidents came while in office, and Benjamin Harrison ran his executive offices from the Congress Hall hotel in the summer of 1891. The town was incorporated as a city in 1851, when its name changed from Cape Island to Cape May City. [@city-history] [@city-about] [@nhl-nomination]

After a fire in 1878 burned 35 acres of the town, Cape May rebuilt on a smaller scale, in Queen Anne, Gothic and bracketed styles. According to the city, deciding against a race with fashionable modern resorts is what kept the old-fashioned look that still draws visitors. The landmark nomination counts more than 600 summer houses, old hotels and shops and calls the town "a kind of textbook of vernacular American building," much of it designed by local carpenters working from pattern books. [@city-history] [@nhl-nomination]

## Why it matters

In its 1976 landmark nomination, the National Park Service described Cape May as having one of the largest collections of late-1800s frame buildings left in the United States. By the early 1900s Atlantic City had become the more fashionable destination, and Cape May struggled to keep up. After a 1962 nor'easter wrecked the boardwalk and beachfront, the city won an urban renewal grant in 1963, and a fight followed between those who wanted to tear down old buildings for modern motels and preservationists who wanted to save them. [@nhl-nomination] [@city-history]

Preservationists prevailed. As the grant required, a Historic American Buildings Survey team led by architectural historian Carolyn Pitts catalogued the old buildings, and in 1970 the town was listed on the National Register of Historic Places. It was designated a National Historic Landmark in 1976. Business owners then brought many of the old houses back to life as inns, guest houses, restaurants and shops, and the city says it now has the second-largest collection of Victorian houses in the country, after San Francisco. [@city-history] [@nhl-nomination] [@nrhp-record] [@nhl-list] [@city-about]

## Worth a visit?

Yes. Cape May is a public city where tourism is a leading industry, with the Washington Street Mall, a beachfront promenade and beaches. Tours of the historic district and some Victorian inns run through the Mid-Atlantic Center for the Arts, and spring and fall bird migrations draw birdwatchers. Check the city's official site for events and visitor information. [@city-about] [@county-cape-may]

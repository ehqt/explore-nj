"Garden State" is the nickname New Jersey put on its license plates in the 1950s, and it shows up in names like the Garden State Parkway. Where it came from is less settled. [@njsl-slogan] [@nj-nickname] [@pl-2017-c214]

The usual story credits Abraham Browning of Camden. The state's official website, drawing on Alfred Heston's 1926 book Jersey Waggon Jaunts, dates this to Browning's remarks at the Philadelphia Centennial exhibition on New Jersey Day, August 24, 1876. In the site's retelling, he compared the state to a barrel "filled with good things to eat and open at both ends," which people from Pennsylvania and New York helped themselves to from opposite sides. The same page notes that Benjamin Franklin is credited with a similar barrel comparison, which some use to question whether Browning deserves credit for the name. [@nj-nickname]

The New Jersey State Library treats the Browning story as "lore" and says the phrase "Garden State" apparently never appears in his speech. By the library's account, the term was already used for New Jersey in an advertisement in the Camden Democrat on March 8, 1862, and again in a Philadelphia Times write-up of the fair on August 25, 1876. The 2017 law that made the slogan official still credits Browning with first making the phrase popular. [@njsl-slogan] [@pl-2017-c214]

Getting the words onto license plates took a veto override. The State Library says Governors Alfred Driscoll and Robert Meyner both vetoed the proposal. Meyner found "no official recognition of the slogan Garden State," and said he did not think the average resident saw the state as "more peculiarly identifiable with gardening" than with its other industries. The legislature overrode his veto by a two-thirds vote in both houses, and the law requiring "Garden State" on passenger license plates passed on December 6, 1954. [@njsl-slogan] [@nj-nickname]

Even so, the State Library notes that the phrase was not officially designated by law until 2017. A law approved on August 7, 2017, names "Garden State" as the New Jersey State Slogan. [@pl-2017-c214] [@njsl-slogan]

## Why it matters

The nickname still points at something real. The U.S. Department of Agriculture's 2025 overview counts about 9,900 farm operations in New Jersey working about 690,000 acres, with crops such as blueberries, bell peppers, peaches, asparagus and cranberries. [@nass-overview]

The Pinelands Commission says New Jersey is among the top states for blueberries and cranberries, and that "virtually all" of them grow in the Pinelands. [@pinelands-people]

The state's beach communities are commonly called "the shore," or the Jersey Shore, and Monmouth University's polls use both names. When the Monmouth University Polling Institute asked residents in 2011 to name a favorite Jersey Shore town, the answers included Sandy Hook, Long Branch, Long Beach Island, Atlantic City, Ocean City, the Wildwoods and Cape May. [@monmouth-2011]

A 2002 state fact sheet put New Jersey's Atlantic coastline at 127 miles, and noted that the coast also takes in the shores of New York/New Jersey Harbor, Raritan Bay and Delaware Bay. [@dep-fact3] [@dep-fact2]

The trip is commonly described as going "down the shore," and the phrase turns up even in formal writing. Monmouth's 2008 and 2011 poll reports use it repeatedly, and some respondents in 2011 volunteered that they "live down shore." Governor Phil Murphy, vetoing a bill to charge a fee for single-use carryout bags, wrote that single-use bags were capable of "ruining a day down the shore." [@monmouth-2008] [@monmouth-2011] [@veto-a3267]

Getting onto the sand can cost money. A 2008 Monmouth report noted that many of the state's beaches require beach tags or a fee. As state law read when it was quoted in a 2024 Senate bill, a town on the ocean or on tidal bays or rivers could charge "reasonable fees" for beach access to pay for upkeep, policing, erosion protection and lifeguards, but could not charge children under 12. The bill calls the pass a "municipal beach tag"; Long Beach Township calls them beach badges. [@monmouth-2008] [@bill-s1895] [@lbt-badges]

At Long Beach Township, badges are per person and per town. For the 2026 summer season, the township required its own badge for everyone 12 and older on its beaches, posted checkers at the street tops and on the beaches, and warned that its badges were not valid in Barnegat Light, Harvey Cedars, Surf City, Ship Bottom or Beach Haven, whose badges are bought from their own municipal offices. [@lbt-badges]

## Why it matters

In Monmouth's 2011 poll, 65% of residents said they planned a shore visit that summer, including 23% planning to stay a week or more. Favorites split by region: North and Central Jersey residents most often named Point Pleasant Beach, while South Jersey residents picked Ocean City. [@monmouth-2011]

In 2008, Monmouth reported that paying beach fees "remains a sticking point for most." Its poll that year found 65% of New Jerseyans disagreed that beachgoers should have to pay a fee, and one in five shore visitors said they had gone onto a beach without paying. "More than 30 years after beach tags began popping up in shore towns, access fees have become a way of life in New Jersey. But that doesn't mean we like paying them," said the institute's director, Patrick Murray. [@monmouth-2008]

A 2002 state fact sheet called coastal tourism "one of the largest industries in the state" and put the contribution of tourism in the four shore counties to the state economy at roughly $16 billion. In 2024, when visitors spent a record $50.6 billion in New Jersey, a study prepared for the state's tourism division found that Atlantic County accounted for 16.2% of that spending, Cape May County 16.0%, Ocean County 11.5% and Monmouth County 6.1%. [@dep-fact3] [@njtt-2024-press] [@njtt-2024-study]

The Monmouth University Polling Institute has more than once asked New Jersey residents a simple question: do you call where you live North, Central, or South Jersey? In a poll taken February 29 to March 4, 2024, 41% answered North, 34% Central and 23% South. A January 2008 Monmouth poll asking a similar question found 40%, 35% and 23%. [@monmouth-2024] [@monmouth-2008]

The two halves are commonly described as facing different big cities. Monmouth's 2008 release pictured the more populous north as "geared toward New York City" and the south as "tuned into Philadelphia," recalling a phrase it credits to Ben Franklin, a "barrel tapped at both ends." The same release traced the divided identity to the late 1600s, when Lord Berkeley and Sir George Carteret split the colony into east and west. Richard Veit, identified in the release as an associate professor of anthropology at Monmouth University, said, "We tend to see ourselves in relation to either New York City or Philadelphia," a pull he said shows in "language, food, and sports team affiliation." [@monmouth-2008]

Where one region stops is its own puzzle. Monmouth's 2008 release said "no one seems to agree on where the northern half ends and the southern half begins." Its 2008 poll listed ten towns near the old colonial dividing line and asked people whether each belonged to North or South Jersey. A majority agreed on only two: 67% put Long Beach Island in South Jersey, and 67% said the same of Toms River. New Brunswick drew 38% for North and 23% for South, while 34% answered Central Jersey even though the question offered only two choices. [@monmouth-2008]

Answers depended heavily on where respondents lived. People in the eight northernmost counties tended to put nearly all ten towns in South Jersey, and people in the eight southernmost counties tended to put most of them in North Jersey. Poll director Patrick Murray described the middle as "less a dividing line than a transitional zone called Central Jersey." [@monmouth-2008]

For its own analysis, Monmouth groups counties this way. North: Bergen, Essex, Hudson, Morris, Passaic, Sussex, Union and Warren. Central: Hunterdon, Mercer, Middlesex, Monmouth and Somerset. South: Atlantic, Burlington, Camden, Cape May, Cumberland, Gloucester, Ocean and Salem. In 2024, nearly 9 in 10 residents described their home region the way Monmouth does. The two sizable exceptions were Union County, where about half of residents say they live in Central Jersey, and Ocean County, where about half see themselves as Central Jerseyans. [@monmouth-2008] [@monmouth-2024]

Whether a middle region exists at all has itself been a subject of debate. According to WHYY, the question got more attention after Governor Phil Murphy appeared on the Late Show with Stephen Colbert in 2018, and a year later he tweeted "CENTRAL JERSEY DOES EXIST" along with a map. [@whyy-2023] [@governor-2023]

On August 24, 2023, Murphy signed a law telling the state Division of Travel and Tourism to redraw the state tourism map with a "Central Jersey" region made up, at a minimum, of Hunterdon, Mercer, Middlesex and Somerset counties, and to use that region in its marketing, including on VisitNJ.org. "Today, we settle the decades-old debate once and for all: Central Jersey exists," the governor said. [@pl-2023-c149] [@governor-2023]

That tourism map is a different scheme from the poll groupings above: the law's minimum list has four counties, while Monmouth's Central Jersey has five. Commenting on the 2024 poll, Murray said, "Now that there is official recognition Central Jersey exists, it seems everyone wants to live there. Or at least say they live there." [@pl-2023-c149] [@monmouth-2024]

## Why it matters

The labels carry feelings about fairness, not just geography. In Monmouth's 2024 poll, 64% of South Jersey residents said their region does not get its fair share of state resources, compared with 45% in North Jersey and 36% in Central Jersey. "South Jersey feels left out and North Jersey doesn't seem to mind," Murray said of the results. [@monmouth-2024]

They show up in everyday habits too. In the same poll, 79% of South Jersey residents named Wawa as their favorite convenience store, compared with 61% in Central Jersey and 31% in North Jersey. [@monmouth-2024]
